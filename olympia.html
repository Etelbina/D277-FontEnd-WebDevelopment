Olympia

At the southern tip of Puget Sound, Olympia is Washington's capital, where Coastal Salish heritage, history, and the waterfront come together.

Overview

Olympia is Washington's capital and one of the oldest American settlements on Puget Sound. Located on the peninsula known as Steh-Chass at the southern end of Budd Inlet, it served for generations as a gathering place for the Nisqually, Squaxin, and other Coast Salish peoples. Settlers Levi Lathrop Smith and Edmund Sylvester claimed the area in 1846 and platted the town in 1850, naming it after the nearby Olympic Mountains. Olympia became the provisional territorial capital in 1853, the permanent capital in 1855, and remained the capital when Washington became the 42nd state on November 11, 1889.

What Visitors Can Expect

Visitors will find a walkable waterfront city where history, government, and nature meet. Olympia developed as a center of maritime commerce through the Mosquito Fleet steamboat system, supported by lumber mills, brick yards, and shipyards before state government became its primary industry. Today, visitors can explore Capitol Campus, the downtown waterfront, historic neighborhoods, museums, and parks while experiencing a community influenced by Coast Salish traditions, early immigrant communities, and institutions such as The Evergreen State College.
