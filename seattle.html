Seattle

Built on hills between two bodies of water, Seattle is where Puget Sound meets Lake Washington beneath the Olympic and Cascade Mountains.

Overview

Seattle is located on the traditional lands of the Coast Salish peoples, particularly the Duwamish and Suquamish. Founded by settlers in 1851 and named after Chief Sealth, the city grew from Henry Yesler's sawmill into a major trade and shipping center for the Pacific Northwest, Alaska, and Asia. Industries such as lumber, shipbuilding, aviation, and later technology helped shape Seattle into one of the nation's leading economic hubs. The city was officially incorporated on December 2, 1869.

What Visitors Can Expect

Visitors can experience a city built on hills, surrounded by water, and known for its mild marine climate and abundant greenery. Seattle offers a vibrant mix of arts, live theater, professional sports, and diverse cultural influences, including Scandinavian, African American, Japanese, Chinese, Filipino, Italian, and Jewish communities. Popular destinations include Pioneer Square, Pike Place Market, Seattle Center, and the International District.
